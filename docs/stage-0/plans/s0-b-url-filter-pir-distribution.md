## Kill-risk question

Can the iOS 26 URL Filter contract support a credible accountless consumer path that uses one canonical reserved blockset to produce Apple-compatible Bloom and exact PIR artifacts, lets the Apple system enforce exact denies across the documented coverage boundary, resolves every Bloom false positive to allow, fails open during indeterminate or unavailable states, preserves private OHTTP/PIR/Privacy Pass boundaries, rolls back safely, expires protocol-correlatable `User-Identifier` state, and works through an approved physical distribution path rather than only a direct-Xcode development bypass?

The answer is negative if any required exact deny cannot be enforced by the system on the supported path; any Bloom false positive blocks; a Bloom/PIR skew, rollback, removal, outage, or corrupt artifact creates an unsafe deny; the app or extension receives a passive URL or block event; the PIR path learns a clear query/result or obtains a prohibited identity; the accountless bearer-token lifecycle is not accepted by the actual Apple distribution path; `User-Identifier` state cannot meet reviewed inactivity and absolute expiry; documented client coverage cannot be stated truthfully; or the physical distribution lifecycle depends on a development-only relaxation. A negative answer pauses Stage 7 and passive-protection claims. Manual paste, share, and QR checks remain a separate product path, subject to the owner outcome for O-014 and any Accepted rescope ADR.

## Scope

### In scope

- Freeze the iOS 26 URL Filter contract for the containing app, control extension, manager configuration, prefilter lifecycle, system enforcement boundary, status handling, and consumer fail-open behavior described in [document 07](../../07-apple-platform.md).
- Validate the checked-in reserved-domain seed and derive Bloom and PIR artifacts from the same canonical manifest only after the proof location, tool, dependency, and handling decisions are authorized.
- Use Apple's `BloomFilterTool` output as the byte-level oracle for FNV-1a/MurmurHash3 double hashing, parameters, seed behavior, bit ordering, and final artifact bytes; an alternative generator is acceptable only if it conforms byte for byte to the frozen official-tool fixture.
- Exercise exact allow, exact deny, measured Bloom false-positive/PIR allow, Bloom miss, invalid or unknown, artifact fetch, freshness, cache, parameter, publication, rollback, kill-switch, and dependency-outage behavior with reserved or generated fixtures.
- Exercise the documented automatic and voluntary coverage boundary for Safari, `WKWebView`, `URLSession`, a voluntarily participating client, and a deliberately nonparticipating stack without claiming broader traffic interception.
- Exercise only the S0-B-facing accountless token boundary: first issue, configuration, rotation, refresh, reinstall, revocation, reduced-trust or unavailable App Attest outcomes, issuer failure, and throttling. Full App Attest cryptographic verification remains S0-C scope.
- Define and exercise only after an Accepted contract the currently unresolved app bootstrap/refresh interface and the control extension's artifact-download authorization, including whether a purpose-limited `filter_download` credential exists, how it reaches the extension, how it rotates/expires, and how it avoids App Attest material, a permanent account, or a shared production secret.
- Prove that App Attest runs only in the containing app, no App Attest material enters the extension, and any downstream bootstrap capability is short-lived, single-purpose, and subjectless at the PIR-auth boundary.
- Exercise Apple OHTTP relay, PIR endpoints, Privacy Pass issuance, source-IP separation, protocol-required `User-Identifier` and evaluation-key state, origin validation, onboarding dependencies, and TestFlight on physical iOS 26 devices after external authorization.
- Measure false-positive behavior, latency, artifact cadence and resource use, load, availability, capacity, recovery, evaluation-key storage, and protocol-state expiry against the proposed proof targets without turning those targets into consumer or production claims.
- Record public-safe metadata only and close every run with teardown or a separately authorized productionization plan.

- Publishing or recording Apple team, application, extension, use-case, App Group, account, signing, profile, certificate, submission, contact, or device identifiers; signing material; service origins; DNS values tied to the project; bearer tokens; attestations; device logs; screenshots; captures; or raw proof output.

- Proving App Attest certificate, nonce, assertion, body-binding, counter, or replay correctness; those belong to S0-C. S0-B proves only its boundary and failure contract with the token bootstrap.
- Inventing the bootstrap/refresh API, a `filter_download` credential, its audience/purpose/digest contract, a containing-app-to-extension handoff, or a signed generation-manifest format. Each is an unresolved prerequisite for an Accepted ADR/contract, not an implementation default in this plan.

- Accepting O-002, O-004, O-005, O-010, O-014, O-018, O-020, any ADR, any external state, or any gate outcome.

### Assumptions and unknowns

- The iOS 26 contract in document 07 remains accurate for the exact approved SDK, OS point releases, Apple samples, and onboarding flow used by the proof. A conflicting signed entitlement, runtime result, primary-source change, or Apple response falsifies this assumption and requires review before continuing.
- The checked-in seed is a deterministic, public-safe canonical input with exactly 1,000 unique reserved `.test` keys. A digest, byte-count, grammar, ordering, encoding, or safety mismatch falsifies this assumption.
- Apple's stock `BloomFilterTool` can produce a valid oracle artifact, but its current public initializer chooses `murmurSeed` randomly and the CLI exposes no seed input. Capture one approved oracle's generated seed and complete parameters; do not require separate stock-tool runs to be byte-identical. The approved proof must select a deterministic generator or adapter, and it may claim reproducibility only when given that exact captured seed and when its indices and bytes conform to the oracle. Unavailable redistribution authority, unexplained version drift, invalid structure or membership behavior, or deterministic-generator disagreement falsifies this assumption.
- Apple's current stock-tool false-positive-tolerance default is `0.001`, which cannot satisfy Hezo's proposed `0.00001` target. The generation contract and invocation must set the reviewed value explicitly and record it; a defaulted value cannot pass by implication.
- Bloom and PIR can be derived from one canonical manifest without widening exact/path scope. Any canonicalization disagreement, wildcard or regex dependency, host-wide broadening, Punycode mismatch, or artifact generation from different input bytes falsifies this assumption.
- A Bloom positive is only a private-lookup trigger and every exact-absent result allows. Any Bloom-only deny or false-positive block falsifies this assumption.
- The system, not app or extension code, can enforce covered HTTP(S) URL requests while revealing no passive URL, exact result, or block callback to Hezo code. Any such observation by the containing app or extension falsifies this assumption.
- The proposed accountless bearer-token lifecycle can satisfy Apple's actual Privacy Pass issuer and distribution behavior without a permanent account or cross-purpose installation profile. A requirement for an account, shared embedded secret, URL-bearing bootstrap, stable cross-plane identifier, or development-only exception falsifies this assumption.
- No approved bootstrap/refresh API, control-extension artifact-download credential contract, or canonical signed generation-manifest schema currently exists. Any case that needs one remains blocked until an Accepted contract defines exact fields, trust boundaries, freshness, authentication, replay, failure, logging, and teardown; the seed manifest cannot substitute for that contract.
- S0-B may use an explicitly labeled, approved S0-C-shaped stub for independent local/development boundary cases, or consume a separately evidenced S0-C interface result when available. A stub proves no App Attest property, S0-C need not Pass before independent S0-B case execution, and S0-B cannot claim full App Attest correctness.
- OHTTP, PIR, and Privacy Pass together provide the documented separation in the distribution path. Clear query/result disclosure, source-IP disclosure to the prohibited Hezo service layer, bearer-token exposure outside the auth/issuer boundary, or linkable custom metadata falsifies this assumption.
- Protocol-required `User-Identifier` state can be limited to a filter-keyed digest plus opaque evaluation key and deleted together under measured inactivity and absolute deadlines. Recovery that requires indefinite retention, logging, export, analytics, or a cross-plane join falsifies this assumption.
- Consumer V1 remains fail open with `shouldFailClosed` explicitly false. Any indeterminate dependency failure that blocks ordinary browsing, or any degraded state recorded as a clean intelligence observation, falsifies this assumption.
- The current product floor says iOS 26. Apple documents tighter production-origin validation beginning in iOS 26.4, including removal of custom-path support, but does not document 26.4 as the first supported distribution release. The proof must run an empirical point-release compatibility matrix rather than infer support or nonsupport for iOS 26.0 through 26.3. A favorable later result does not prove an earlier cell; an unsupported floor requires an Accepted minimum-OS rescope before Pass.
- TestFlight is a known production App Attest environment. Whether the exact URL Filter build traverses the required Apple OHTTP relay and accepted origin path is an S0-B distribution hypothesis, not a premise. The physical distribution cases must observe that path and rule out direct-Xcode relaxations while every Hezo-controlled service, credential, dataset, and account remains disposable and proof-only.
- The proposed performance and availability values in [document 10](../../10-testing-and-benchmarks.md#performance-load-and-availability-targets) are release targets, not automatically S0-B feasibility gates. Bounded Stage 0 observations may compare against them but cannot claim an SLA or monthly availability result. A short or confounded optional observation is recorded as a non-favorable bounded limitation, not a root Inconclusive outcome, unless the same instrumentation gap prevents a separately identified required safety, privacy, fail-open, or correctness invariant from being decided.

## Environment and isolation

No environment, runner, project, dependency, workflow, route, device, service, or evidence location is authorized by this Draft. Before approval, an Accepted decision must freeze the following three bounded classes without exposing restricted details in Git.

### Local/offline contract class

- Read only declared repository fixtures and approved pinned tool bytes from an authorized disposable location.
- Deny DNS, sockets, loopback, remote schema/tool retrieval, Apple services, production routes, credentials, private roots, and undeclared file access technically.
- Recompute fixture integrity; generate or compare approved official-tool, alternate-generator, canonicalization, Bloom, and PIR artifacts only within the declared resource bounds.
- Record tool/SDK/compiler/fixture revisions and deterministic output digests, but no local filesystem path or machine identity in public evidence.
- Reset generated files, caches, process state, locale, time zone, and temporary mutations before repeats.

### Physical-device development class

- Use only an authorized physical iPhone, reserved candidates, proof-scoped signed artifacts, and an approved local/isolated reference-service boundary; Simulator output is not networking proof.
- The allowlist must name only the exact Apple or proof services needed by a case. All other egress, production routes, production credentials, live-threat access, general browsing, and unrelated app traffic remain unavailable.
- Keep actual device, signing, application, profile, account, network, and service identifiers in restricted evidence. Public metadata may state only the approved physical-device class, exact relevant OS/tool version, install class, sanitized result, and opaque reference.
- Start each lifecycle case from its declared manager, token, Bloom, PIR cache, evaluation-key, extension, application, and network state. A reboot, update, reinstall, clock-change, cache reset, or extension restart is performed only by its named case and is followed by an inventory/reset.

### Physical-device distribution class

- Use a proof-scoped internal TestFlight path on an authorized physical iOS 26 device only after privacy review, external authorization, and exact Apple onboarding state are confirmed. Include iOS 26.4 or later to test Apple's tightened origin rules. External testers are out of scope.
- Treat App Attest under TestFlight as Apple's production environment. Treat URL Filter relay/onboarding behavior as the hypothesis this class must observe and prove, not as an assumed TestFlight property. Every Hezo-controlled bootstrap, issuer, gateway, PIR, artifact, credential, account, dataset, log, and resource remains disposable proof infrastructure with no production data, route, credential, or authority.
- Permit only the Apple system/relay and approved proof-scoped bootstrap, issuer, gateway, and PIR origins required for the named case. The service topology and origins remain restricted and do not gain production authority.
- Prove the distribution request path independently of direct-Xcode relaxations, including HTTPS/domain-only/standard-port/no-path/no-trailing-slash origin rules and HTTP/2 gateway negotiation.
- Observe source-IP, authorization, `User-Identifier`, evaluation-key, query/result, and logging boundaries at every approved layer with synthetic canaries. A policy statement or code review is not evidence that defaults were suppressed.
- Freeze exact request, concurrency, storage, time, retry, load, cost, and retention bounds before execution. Exceeding any bound stops the case; a retry may not widen access or create a new external submission.
- Reset or delete manager configuration, proof app/extension, tokens, evaluation keys, `User-Identifier` digests, PIR caches, service state, logs, build artifacts, and temporary external resources under the reviewed closeout process.

The approved matrix must substantiate the product's iOS 26.0 floor and separately test the tightened iOS 26.4-or-later production-origin rule. Apple has not documented 26.4 as the first supported distribution release. No result may be projected into an untested point release; an unsupported floor requires an Accepted rescope before Pass.

The public evidence must never identify the device, Apple account/team, application/extension, signing/profile, TestFlight group, service origin, network, or human operator. The restricted reviewer must still be able to verify those exact values and the signed artifacts through the authorized private evidence index.

- the containing app or control extension receives a passive URL, PIR result, block callback, or browsing history; the extension attempts App Attest; or a prohibited cross-plane join succeeds;

- evidence is incomplete, corrupted, unreproducible, stale, unsafe to retain, inconsistent with contact counters, or unavailable to an authorized reviewer.

Immediate containment is to halt traffic and distribution activity; fail open; disable the affected proof configuration or generation through the approved mechanism; revoke proof credentials/tokens; isolate affected logs/state; notify the accountable security/privacy/iOS/external-submission roles privately; and execute `S0-B-CASE-900`. A Stop result may answer the kill-risk question but is never a passed gate.

### Inconclusive outcome

The result is Inconclusive when an already-authorized run cannot distinguish a required gating outcome—for example, Apple onboarding or a required service is temporarily unavailable; the system observer cannot prove relay versus direct routing or system versus app enforcement; the approved false-positive sample is statistically insufficient; an OS/tool change invalidates the frozen oracle; a device lifecycle result cannot be separated from unrelated platform state; a privacy canary cannot distinguish absence from missing instrumentation; or the exact external topology remains ambiguous. A short or confounded `S0-B-CASE-112`, `S0-B-CASE-113`, `S0-B-CASE-115`, or `S0-B-CASE-116` observation is a bounded non-favorable limitation and does not make the root result Inconclusive unless it also prevents a separately named required safety, privacy, fail-open, capacity-safety, or correctness case from being decided.

Before authorization, a missing owner decision, external approval, environment, project, tool, device, or evidence location leaves the plan Draft; it is not an Inconclusive execution. After an inconclusive run, fail open, complete `S0-B-CASE-900`, preserve the limitation, revise and reapprove the plan, freeze a new revision/matrix, and rerun every affected case. Partial development success cannot be rounded up to distribution credibility.
